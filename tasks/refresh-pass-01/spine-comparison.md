## Finding

The proposed expansion fits the existing argument if it preserves two obligations together: develop capable AI-supported work and make human purpose, reliance, and responsibility observable. A version focused only on detecting weak student understanding would lose the performance ambition of the essay. A version focused only on better production would lose the educational argument.

The largest extension is developmental: teach and observe the knowledge and reasoning that the existing method expects learners to exercise. The strongest qualification concerns frame ownership: educators may supply a frame while learners practice consequential choices within it. The expected scope of learner authority changes; human responsibility remains explicit.

## Argument order for the foundation brief

Working claim: education needs observable human judgment inside increasingly capable AI-supported work.

Sharper thesis: educators should build the knowledge and reasoning learners need, expand the consequential choices they can own, and assess both supported performance and their ability to explain and adapt it.

The mistaken inference to challenge is that a good finished product establishes either understanding or effective direction of AI. Its converse also needs challenging: independent performance alone does not establish skill at directing AI-supported work.

1. Begin with a teacher observing stronger work and asking what the learner can now explain or do. Include inherited AI-shaped material, not only direct chatbot use. (Dependencies 1–2.)
2. Establish owned purpose and human responsibility as the educational design obligation. Identify frame, reliance, and transfer as observable practices. (Dependencies 3–5.)
3. Explain how educators build the prerequisite knowledge and reasoning, while expanding the decisions learners can own. Preserve useful effort and teach missing strategies. (Dependency 6; developmental extension.)
4. Show how a changed task, questioning, and a lean revision record can test the claim of learning. (Dependency 7.)
5. End with educators practicing the same discipline and saving reviewed improvements. (Dependency 8.)

Retain: “AI-assisted performance is real performance”; purpose and accountability as obligations; consequential decisions as inspectable evidence. Avoid: all AI use as suspicion, universal unaided-first rules, every learner as a future agent supervisor, and better AI output as automatic learning gain.

## Changes made because of the comparison

- Kept AI-enabled performance and directing useful assistance explicit, including accepting a contribution for good reasons.
- Included inherited summaries and teacher-selected AI material, so direct student AI use is not required for the method to apply.
- Added readiness checks and support without weakening the eventual expectation of independent reasoning where needed.
- Assigned different kinds of framing to each exercise, instead of requiring every student to originate the whole problem.
- Kept educator practice, calibration, and reviewed reuse in every sketch.
- Preserved failure modes beyond factual error: narrow success criteria, evidence selection, and unsupported inference.

## Unresolved tests

The strongest challenge is that these exercises may reward a practiced explanation of ownership without developing transferable understanding. The changed task must therefore demand an actual new decision or performance. Even that is only local evidence; durable transfer needs later observation.

A second challenge is usefulness relative to existing teaching. Educators should compare the proposed activity with what they already do, including its preparation and review burden. If the extra trace reveals nothing useful, reduce it or remove it. If AI adds no educational value to a task, change its role or keep the activity without it.

Editorial/design finding: the drafts below preserve the spine with explicit qualifications. Evidence finding: the expanded pedagogy and the three exercises remain proposals requiring disciplinary, developmental, and classroom review.
