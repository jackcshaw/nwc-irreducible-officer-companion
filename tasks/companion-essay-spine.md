# Companion essay adaptation spine

Approved direction: adapt The Irreducible Officer directly for HE and high-school educators, preserving its eleven-section argument and eight claims. Original essay and canonical claim map remain unchanged.

Thesis: AI can improve work while obscuring a learner's contribution. Educators must teach the knowledge needed to direct assistance and observe consequential decisions, including responses to changed conditions.

Core correction: output quality, disclosed tool use, and learning are different observations. Strong unaided performance and strong assisted performance both matter where the task requires them.

Argument: changed performance standard; two learners and seven failures; limits of finished work; human authorization of purpose; appropriate reliance; developmental effort; role-bounded responsibility; observable assessment; a small interactive pilot; educator practice and shared materials; return to the learners.

HE: discipline-specific evidence, undergraduate readiness never assumed; supplied frame legitimate; advanced orchestration optional. Fictional survey illustrates nonresponse versus random sampling.
High school: knowledge and modeling explicit in opening; teacher decides scope; students own bounded inferences; adults own access, materials, assessment and school decisions. Fictional surface-temperature comparison illustrates confounding.

Evidence: reuse original source notes with scope preserved. Bastani high-school mathematics does not establish HE or all-school effects. EEF supports subject-embedded modeling, not a validated AI progression. Aviation and command examples remain original PME context, not evidence of classroom outcomes. New pilots are proposals.

Objections: students need knowledge to critique; strict tasks can teach; explanations can be rehearsed; assessment can overwhelm teachers; access supports must persist; constructive AI use must be observable; changed condition is near transfer, not durable transfer.

Keep: purpose/frame/reliance/accountability/transfer distinctions; seven failure names; finished-product paragraph; invisible-delegation paragraph; effort-as-learning distinction; institutional artifacts with visible assumptions.
Avoid: literal AI inability to suggest goals; universal independent framing; wasted search/drafting labels; command responsibility assigned to children; automatic competence judgments; invented evidence or participant responses.

Opening: two learners' strong products, then inspect their decisions. Bring high-school subject prerequisites into that scene immediately.
