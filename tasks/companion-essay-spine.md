# Companion essay adaptation spine

Revised September 25, 2026, after Jack's review of the first testing drafts. Supersedes the earlier foundations-first spine. The original essay and canonical claim map remain unchanged.

## What changed and why

The first drafts made foundations the destination and left critique as the only AI activity. The original essay argues the opposite: AI raised the bar, and the second officer's method is the standard to meet. Jack confirmed the goal is learners who direct AI well. Foundations belong inside the loop, not as a gate in front of it.

## Thesis (same as the original)

The goal is learners who direct AI toward a purpose they own, rely on it selectively, and can defend the result. A polished product hides whether they did. Educators have to make that visible and teach it.

## Decisions

- **Position, not hedging.** One short scope paragraph near the top; the essays then argue plainly. People can disagree.
- **Opening scene.** Restore the original contrast. The AI-directing student is the model, not the suspect. Restore the harder case: the capable unaided student who looks finished.
- **Directing AI well has levels.** Use the Section V progression: minimal prompting, structured prompting, evaluator loops, reusable workflows and multiple agents. HE reaches the top for advanced students; high school works at structured prompting and evaluator loops, with the teacher running the tool where students lack accounts.
- **Pilot.** Restore the original five steps: unaided frame (readiness lives here), student directs AI against the frame, misframed AI answer, diagnose and revise, defend and meet the changed case.
- **Examples pass the five tests** in `artifacts/frame-first-assignment-design.md`.
- **Failure modes.** Only those the example shows.
- **Headings** describe each edition's argument, not the original's.
- **Accountability.** The person who sets the standard answers for the result, at every role. No rebuttal of a position nobody holds.
- **Asymmetry.** HE and high school differ where their material differs.
- **Product and facilitator text** moves out of the essays into the guides and facilitator.

## HE: return-to-office research memo

A student writes a memo for a firm deciding whether to require office work. AI synthesizes the research into a fluent "evidence is mixed; hybrid is the balance" answer. Its summaries are accurate: Ctrip home-working volunteers performed 13% better but were promoted less at equal performance (Bloom et al., 2015); Trip.com hybrid cut quits by a third with no effect on performance ratings (Bloom, Han & Liang, 2024); junior engineers lost feedback when teammates were not nearby (Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais). The frame flaw: "productivity" silently means short-run output averaged across workers. For a firm hiring mostly new graduates, development and promotion evidence decides the question. Changed case: an experienced call-center workforce makes different evidence decisive.

Failure modes shown: frame capture, fluency substitution, premature synthesis, invisible delegation, institutional monoculture. Translation note for other disciplines: historiography that averages incompatible questions; a design review that optimizes the wrong requirement.

## High school: "Was the New Deal a success?"

AI writes a balanced essay judging success by recovery and durability. Its facts are right: unemployment about 25% in 1933, about 14% in 1937, 19% in 1938; lasting programs such as Social Security. It never asks success for whom. Social Security first excluded agricultural and domestic workers, occupations that included at least 60% of Black Americans (DeWitt, SSA Bulletin, 2010); historians dispute whether racial politics or administrative feasibility drove it. Students direct AI to argue assigned perspectives (factory worker, sharecropper, business owner), check each against the teacher's document packet, and defend a standard. Invented role-play detail is the reliance lesson. Changed case: apply the standard-setting move to a current local policy.

Failure modes shown: frame capture, fluency substitution, invisible delegation, institutional monoculture. The teacher sets the lesson's purpose; the student sets the argument's standard.

## Evidence

## Avoid

Strawman AI outputs; template-solvable flaws; inspect-only AI activity; repeated caveats; interpretive morals after examples; mirrored sections across editions; product names in the essays.
